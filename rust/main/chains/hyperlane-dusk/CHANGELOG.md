# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Changed

- The agent looked a transaction it waits for up as often as a block can bring it: at once, again one block (10 s) later, as a transaction just sent is in a block no sooner, every 2.5 s until 20 s had passed, and then after a quarter of the time waited, at most every 10 s. It looked one up every 2 s: over the 120 s wait that is 17 lookups instead of 61, and a transaction in a block 10 to 20 s after it was sent, as Rusk includes one, is found by at most 6 instead of up to 11, at most 2.5 s after its block. That wait follows pipelined deliveries, whose `tx` reads the benchmark counted at 9.3 a delivery, a transaction whose outcome is unknown, and the receipt once `dusk-tx` returns, which it finds at once. `dusk-tx` itself, which waits for a delivery sent one at a time, still looks it up right after propagating it and every 3 s, about 6 `tx` reads a delivery.
- Every contract kept the heights of the final records its searches read, of the 4,096 most recent blocks with records they read, and a search below the last final height searched, such as the block of a transaction that `txIdIndexingEnabled` looks up for each new message's IGP payment and Merkle insertion, looked only between the closest kept records on either side of it. Such a search used to read the records below the last final height again: through a multicall, the IGP's lookups read about 100 `gas_payment_at` a Dusk to EVM message after 100 payments and 295 after 10,000, and a restart with 40 of 100 messages waiting read 8,100; now a message's lookups read none once the indexer has read its block, whatever the history, and that restart reads each payment's height once (`tests/node_load.rs`). Without a multicall a message's lookups read 4 payment heights and 2 counts, now 1 and 1.
- Pipelined deliveries are experimental and off by default. A relayer sends every Dusk delivery one at a time, as before they existed: `dusk-tx` signs it with the account's nonce plus one, as the node reports it, and waits for its block, the agent chooses and keeps no nonce, and an unknown outcome is followed by the prepared transaction's hash. A delivery submitted again while its first transaction waits in the mempool is refused by the node at preverification. Only a chain that sets the new `pipelineDeliveries: true` and a `maxBatchSize` above 1 pipelines; a configuration that only carries `maxBatchSize` delivers one at a time, and the deploy scripts no longer write `maxBatchSize`. The agent logs which way it delivers once at startup.
- Turning pipelined deliveries off after a pipelined run needs no manual step. Before a delivery goes one at a time, the relayer looks at the deliveries the pipelined run left in flight in `eventCursorDir` (with none, it reads nothing): while one may still be admitted ahead of it, because its transaction is in the mempool, it was sent within the 60 s drop grace, the mempool holds its nonce for an unnamed transaction or it cannot be looked up, the delivery waits and the relayer retries later. One whose transaction is in a final block, or that can no longer be admitted, is forgotten; one in a block that is not final yet is kept until it is, without holding anything up.
- A chain whose `batchContractAddress` names Multicall for Dusk (mainnet and testnet run it at `93de287b7d349d678a0038fe7772e8960e1a84a1b5da2d0b1f62f532616ce1d4`) read contract state through it: reads that do not depend on each other went in one `aggregate` query, answered at one state, in runs of at most 256 calls whose argument and reply fit its 64 KiB even if every call fails, and an aggregate was one request to the pace and to the node's rate limit. The deployment check took one aggregate, after a probe of `version` and an empty `aggregate`, instead of ten queries. The counts and delivery checks read at each final block took one aggregate, so an idle relayer sent 30 requests a minute instead of 48. Indexers read a range's heights, IDs and pages together and searched record heights 256 at a time, so a restart's catch-up of 1,000 records sent 25 contract queries instead of 3,048, and of 10,000 records 151 instead of 30,132. A validator's checks against the hook (`count`, `root`, `tree` and `message_id_at` of the last leaf) and the last pages of IDs went in one aggregate at one state, instead of queries that could straddle a block; the hook's own `root` is now checked too, and the state's height must be at or above the block the leaves were counted at. Messages prepared together read their recipient's ISM and its `validators_and_threshold` once, and reads asked while an aggregate was in flight went together in the next. A call that failed gave the error its single query gives, one short of gas was sent again on its own, and an aggregate that failed as a whole was split and sent again, down to single queries. A multicall that failed its probe or answered wrongly was set aside with one warning and probed again every 10 minutes. Without `batchContractAddress`, every read is one query, as before.
- Contract queries were paced per endpoint, for every client of it in an agent process: 100 a second, in bursts of up to 20 and at most 8 at once by default (`contractQueriesPerSecond`, `contractQueryBurst`), inside the 180 a second, 360 in 2 s and 12 at once that Rusk 1.7.3 allows all of a node's clients together. A relayer restarted against a deployment with history sent 887 contract queries in its first 10 s, and the node refused 48, 9 of them the validators'.
- A request the node refused for its rate limit (`429`) was sent again after the refusal's `Retry-After` plus up to as long again at random, doubling with each refusal in a row, up to 4 times, while every request of its class from the process waited out the refusal too. It used to fail at once, and the relayer's three indexers then backed off 5, 10, 20, ... 300 s in step and came back together.
- Every client of an endpoint in a process read the node's tip and last final block from one `lastBlockPair` read per 5 s tick, and kept what it computed from final data for the last final block it was computed at: the indexers' counts and tips, checkpoints, `delivered` and the announcement checks. Until the node finalized another block they cost no request, the relayer's `delivered` checks every half second while it waits for signatures included; then each record indexer read its contract's count once and the heights of the records past the last final one it knew, galloping from there, and a validator's indexer and checkpoint reader shared that read. An idle relayer with 100 messages of history sent 305 requests a minute, 71% of them binary searches over every record's height, and each validator 109; at any history they now send 48 and 18 on a 10 s chain. Finality is seen up to a tick later.
- The delivery log read the archive only once the node had finalized a block past the ones it holds, and a checkpoint whose leaf count had not changed was not checked against the hook again: their leaves and rows are final.
- MerkleTreeHook insertion indexing read the stored IDs a page of 256 at a time (`message_ids`), instead of one `message_id_at` per insertion.
- A `process` simulation that passed less than 2 s before stood for the next one of the same arguments, so a delivery whose submission followed its preparation ran one simulation instead of three (the relayer's preparation, its dry run before submitting, and `process` itself).
- The backward sequence cursor in `hyperlane-base` started a range above the logs its store already holds below the gap it fills. A relayer restarted after 40 of 99 Dusk messages arrived read all 99 again, at about 46 requests each, because the chunk of 1,999 sequences below the first missing one reached back to sequence 0.
- An endpoint's deployment was checked once per process for every client of it that requires the same deployment. A validator's checkpoint reader, which has its own client, ran the 13-query check a second time.
- With `maxBatchSize` above 1 and a `dusk-tx` that takes `call --nonce <N> --no-wait`, the relayer sent a batch of deliveries as consecutive transactions of its account, each once the node held the one before, and they shared a block. One at a time, a relayer key delivered one message every two blocks, 3 a minute on a 10 s chain: the helper returns once a transaction is in a block, after the next block's round has started.
- The default Dusk `gasLimit` was 5,000,000 instead of 500,000,000. A failed transaction pays its whole limit, and a relayed `process` costs about 1.95M gas plus 0.22M per ISM signature; the deploy scripts already set 5,000,000 for the relayer.
- Dispatch indexing read each message from its finalized `Dispatch` event, matched by the message ID the Mailbox stores (`dispatched_id`), because Mailbox state version 4 no longer stores dispatched messages.

- Finalized-event indexing matched every record of a block in one pass over that block's archive rows, 200 rows per `finalizedEvents` page (Rusk's maximum), instead of rescanning the block from its first row for each record. A block of 256 dispatches took 3 pages instead of 4,224.
- Archive scan positions were kept in `eventCursorDir`, about one per 200 rows, so a restart resumed near the block it needed instead of at the contract's first row. A position was reused only after the archive returned the row it was taken from, unchanged.
- Startup refused a Mailbox below `state_version` 4 with an explicit error, instead of every dispatch fetch failing later with an unexplained HTTP 500.
- Deliveries were indexed from the Mailbox's finalized `ProcessId` events, instead of from the processed-message log that Mailbox state version 5 no longer has. The agent keeps a log of them in `eventCursorDir`, every non-reverted row the archive has finalized, numbered in block order and within a block in archive order, so a delivery's sequence no longer follows execution order within a block. The log reads on from the closest saved scan position, at most 16 pages per indexer call, takes a block's rows only once it has read all of them and `checkBlock(onlyFinalized)` accepted the block, keeps its numbering across restarts and renumbered archives, and reads its blocks again when the endpoint no longer accepts one it served. The indexer's tip is the height up to which the log holds every finalized delivery.
- Announced storage locations were read from ValidatorAnnounce's finalized `ValidatorAnnouncement` events, keeping each location whose keccak256 the contract lists for the validator (`get_announced_storage_location_hashes`), in the list's order; state version 2 keeps no location strings. Relayers see a location once its announcement is final, instead of as soon as the tip held it. A validator's startup check looks for the hash of its own location in its list, so another location listed for it, read or not, no longer holds it up, and it reports the location only once its announcement log holds the announcement in a block `checkBlock(onlyFinalized)` accepts.
- Checkpoint roots and the validator's start tree were computed from the message IDs the MerkleTreeHook stores, instead of read with `root_at`, which hook state version 2 no longer has. The tree is kept between checkpoints and extended with new IDs only, read in pages of 256 whose length is checked. Its last ID is checked against `message_id_at`, and whenever the hook holds exactly its leaves, its count, root and the branch nodes the count's set bits select are checked against the hook's own `tree`. The other branch nodes are not compared: the hook starts them at zero, hyperlane-core at the empty subtrees' hashes. A tree that fails a check is dropped and the read fails.
- Startup required Mailbox `state_version` 5, MerkleTreeHook 2 and ValidatorAnnounce 2, the versions this agent reads, and refused any other, newer ones included. It used to accept any Mailbox from version 4 on, so an agent started against the redesigned contracts and then failed on queries they no longer have.

### Fixed

- A delivery whose transaction failed in a block, its failure reported, could be sent again while that transaction could still land. Submitted again, the delivery looked the transaction up first and read the last final block after: a block rolled back in between, with another block final at its height, counted as the transaction's final block, though the rollback had put the transaction back into the mempool. The transaction was forgotten and the message signed again, and if the first transaction then delivered it, the second failed, paying its whole gas limit. The last final block is now read before the lookup: a block the lookup finds the transaction in, at or below it, is that final block, since a rollback deletes a block's transactions from the ledger the lookup reads. The other places that weigh a transaction's block against finality, the look at kept transactions for room, the pipelined reconciliation and the delivery checks, already read the last final block first.
- A kept delivery transaction could be taken for gone while it was in the node's mempool or a block. The agent looks a transaction up with one query that asks for both, but Rusk answers its `mempoolTx` and `tx` from two separate reads, in either order: a rollback between them, the mempool read before and the ledger read after, or an acceptance with its mempool clean-up between them, the other way round, made the lookup miss it. Submitted again past the drop grace, the delivery then forgot its transaction and had `dusk-tx` sign the message again with the nonce that transaction held, and room-making forgot a transaction a rollback had put back into the mempool, past the rule that keeps those. A lookup that misses a transaction now reads the ledger, the mempool and the ledger again, one after the other, and finds it unless a block holding it is accepted and rolled back between the two ledger reads; and a kept transaction counts as gone, at a delivery or for room, only if a second lookup half a block later misses it too. In Rusk 1.7.3 a rollback puts a block's transactions back into the mempool in the database commit that deletes the block, and an acceptance takes them out of the mempool only in a commit after the one that stores the block, so no single read misses a transaction that stays on the node.
- A relayer restarted while a delivery's transaction waited in the mempool submitted the other ready messages first: each was simulated, signed by `dusk-tx` with the account's nonce plus one, which that transaction held, and refused at preverification ("this transaction's spendId exists in the mempool"), costing a warning, an upstream error and a retry each (15 of them and 205 requests in 10 s in the E2E benchmark). Before a delivery is simulated and signed, it now waits for the transaction kept for the signer's latest delivery of another message to leave the mempool, for at most the receipt wait from its start, the scan of kept transactions and lookups included, signing nothing meanwhile: a lookup still unanswered at its end is given up, and none starts past it, so a reply held past it, even one saying the transaction landed, cannot have the delivery signed late. Only that transaction is looked up, and only until its outcome is known, so a relayer that is not catching up after a restart reads nothing more.
- Kept transactions of messages the relayer gave up on, which never landed, stayed for good: room-making at the bound forgot only transactions in final blocks, and a dropped one went only when its own message was submitted again. Once 1,024 piled up, no delivery could be signed again, even after the signer's account had used their nonces. `dusk-tx` now names the nonce it signed with on its prepared line (`Prepared TX <hash>; nonce <N>; ...`), and the agent keeps it with the hash. A transaction in neither a block nor the node's mempool, whose nonce two readings of the account at least 10 minutes apart both show used, can never execute, and is forgotten: at the bound, and every 5 minutes at a delivery of any message (`SubmissionLimits::nonce_gap`). Rusk reads accounts at its tip only, so a reading is the account between two reads of the tip that agree; the first counts once that tip block is final and still the node's block at its height, and the second is read right before the transaction is looked up. One reading is not enough: a block accepted and rolled back between its reads of the tip, holding the kept transaction, shows in it, while the rollback puts the transaction back into the mempool, from which it can land. A transaction in the mempool always stays. A kept transaction without its nonce, from a `dusk-tx` before it named it, goes only once it is in a final block. Such a transaction goes 10 minutes after a reading first shows its nonce used at the earliest, and about 20 minutes when a reading taken shortly before must age first, plus the wait for the next look. The README's known issues say what the two readings still assume.
- A delivery's transaction that failed in a block after its submission had returned, its outcome unknown, escaped gas accounting. Submitted again, the delivery found it failed, forgot it once its block was final and sent another transaction at once, so the relayer never heard of the gas the first one spent, which is its whole gas limit, and the message's payment kept paying for more attempts. Such a failure is now the outcome of that submission, with its transaction and the gas it spent, and nothing else is sent then: the relayer charges the gas to the message and its gas payment policy decides on another attempt. Each failure reported is noted on the kept transaction (a byte more in `dusk-prepared-delivery-v1`; a record without it, which only builds of 2026-11-16 wrote, reads as reported, so it is never charged twice), so it is reported once, and the next submission sends again once its block is final.
- A search for how many of a contract's records are at or below a final block that found a height contradicting the kept ones still answered from that height, though it had forgotten the kept heights. With records at heights 10, 20, ..., 80, the heights of records 4, 6 and 7 kept, and one wrong reply of 40 for record 5, a search through block 59 answered 6 instead of 5, and block 60 then seemed to hold no record: its IGP payment was skipped. A search whose reads contradict each other, the kept heights or the last final boundary is now not used: the kept heights are forgotten, and a search of every record, reading each height again, decides; when that one contradicts itself too, the search fails, keeping nothing, instead of answering a guess.
- At its bound of 1,024 transactions kept for deliveries, a signer forgot the one prepared longest ago, whatever had become of it, so a transaction that could still land lost the protection that keeps its message from being sent again with another nonce. At the bound, a delivery now looks kept transactions up, at most 16, the oldest first and on later tries on from where the last look stopped, and forgets only those in final blocks, but not one that failed before its failure was reported, whose gas would then never be charged; while none can go, the delivery fails without being signed, and goes once kept transactions settle.
- A delivery's transaction could reach the node before the relayer kept its hash. `dusk-tx` printed the hash and went on to preverify and propagate it while the relayer read it from stderr and kept it: a relayer killed in between left a helper that sent the transaction anyway, and the restarted relayer, with nothing kept, could send the message again with the next nonce, the first then landing and the second failing, paying its whole gas limit. A hash the event store failed to keep was only logged, with the same result. The relayer now runs `dusk-tx call --ack-before-send`, which waits after printing the hash for a line on its stdin: the relayer writes `send` only once the hash is kept, and a helper whose stdin closes or says anything else sends nothing and says `Not submitted:`. A hash that cannot be kept fails the delivery `NotSubmitted`, nothing kept, so the next submission goes at once. The relayer asks a helper's `call --help` once per process and refuses to deliver, naming the `dusk-tx` it needs, when it does not list `--ack-before-send`.
- A delivery sent one at a time could be sent twice. After the helper's outcome was unknown and the relayer's wait for its transaction ended, the relayer submitted the delivery again; its simulation passed, the first transaction landed before `dusk-tx` read the account's nonce, and the helper signed the next nonce: a second transaction landed and failed, paying its whole gas limit. The Mailbox now keeps the hash of the transaction `dusk-tx` prepared for each delivery sent one at a time, as soon as the helper prints it before propagating it (`dusk-prepared-delivery-v1` in `eventCursorDir`, in memory without one, at most 1,024 a signer, until the relayer confirms the message delivered), and looks that transaction up before it submits the delivery again: in a block and executed, it reports that outcome, and the relayer's confirmation goes on from it; failed in a block, the delivery goes again once that block is final; in the mempool, it follows the transaction to its block; in neither, the delivery goes again only once 60 s have passed since the transaction was prepared. A transaction refused at preverification is not kept, and a delivery submitted again while its first transaction waits in the mempool no longer runs the helper into the node's refusal.
- Pipelined deliveries, behind `pipelineDeliveries`, hold every nonce they sign with in a reservation, keyed by the nonce, until one of its transactions is in a final block. A delivery dropped by the node kept its nonce only while it was in flight: a new delivery sent on its own took the same nonce, and once both were lost, a batch of the two kept one and the other vanished from its outcome, neither sent nor left out, so the relayer dropped the message. A nonce is now held once and a message holds one, and every delivery of a batch is either in its outcome or left out.
- A pipelined delivery's nonce was released once its message was delivered in a final block, by anyone, while its own transaction could still be in the mempool; another delivery took a later nonce, and when the node evicted the first and its dependants, the signer was left at its bound of 64 with a free nonce below the dropped ones, which nothing could fill, also after a restart. Delivery no longer releases a nonce. A reservation whose transactions the node dropped goes again with its nonce, with the arguments it kept and a fresh simulation when its message is not in the batch, or a filler (a call of the Mailbox's `state_version`) takes the nonce when that simulation fails; a free nonce below held ones gets a filler. Neither needs room under the bound.
- A pipelined delivery's transaction hash was kept only after the helper had sent it, and a delivery without one was forgotten once the account's nonce passed it at the tip: after a crash between the two, a rollback that put the transaction back into the mempool let the relayer send the message again with a later nonce, and the second paid its whole gas limit. The helper now signs against an endpoint of the agent's own on the loopback interface and sends nothing; the agent keeps the hash, then preverifies and propagates the transaction itself. A reservation is released only on a transaction of it in a block at or below the last final block, read before the transaction.
- The bound of 64 outstanding pipelined deliveries left out those in blocks that were not final, so a rollback could put more than 64 back into the mempool. Every reservation not released counts.
- Two processes signing with one key bypassed the pipelined nonce reservations. A relayer that finds a nonce it holds used by a transaction it did not sign now logs a critical error and delivers one at a time from then on; the README says that a pipelining relayer must be the only process signing with its key.
- A pipelined batch's 60 s to send covered neither the helper nor the reconciliation before it. The reconciliation's reads, the helper's signing, its own sending and the lock now all end by the deadline; a transaction is never sent after it, and a hash kept before an uncertain or interrupted send stays.

- A pipelined batch whose transactions stayed in the mempool past its 120 s wait for their blocks reported no gas and kept neither their transactions nor their nonces: once the relayer's confirmation delay had passed, it prepared the messages again, and the next batch sent them again with new nonces, behind the first. When the chain took them all, the second deliveries failed as already delivered and paid their whole gas limit. A delivery sent one at a time could do the same, signed with a nonce a batch's transaction held. Each delivery's nonce, and then its transaction, are now kept in `eventCursorDir` until the delivery settles, and the signer's deliveries are reconciled with the node before one is sent again, after a restart too: one whose transaction is in the mempool or a block is followed instead of sent again, and one the node dropped goes again with the nonce it owns, so that at most one of its transactions can ever be in a block. A signer has at most 64 deliveries outstanding, sent and in no block yet.
- A delivery check, a validator's startup check and a relayer's read of announced locations were kept for the node's last final block even when their event log could not settle them: after an archive that failed once, or a log that had read only its 4 or 16 pages of a long history, the same answer came back until the node finalized another block. While finality stalled, a validator whose announcement was final could not start, a final delivery did not count, and a relayer read no location of a validator whose announcement its log had read since. Such an answer is now kept for at most 1 s, and only while the log has not read on; one the log settled is kept for the block as before.
- An archive rebuilt without deliveries contract sync had indexed, with no other delivery to take their sequences, left the delivery log with fewer deliveries than contract sync held, and no halt: the published-sequence check ran only on rows read again under those sequences. The scraper then failed every tick with "Provider sequence count is behind durable history". Once the log holds every finalized delivery up to the block of the first published one it lacks, it now halts with the critical error. A read that reaches the archive's end without the blocks the log holds, after the archive had finalized the node's last final block, also drops them, as it drops blocks that differ, instead of keeping rows the archive no longer has.
- A delivery log the previous version wrote, which records no published sequences, counted none of its deliveries as published, and the delivery indexer advanced it before it published its count again: a recovery read during the upgrade, of an archive whose history had changed, could number deliveries contract sync had indexed again without halting. Every delivery such a log holds now counts as published before any read of the archive. The announcement log, which no consumer reads by sequence, still counts none.
- A pipelined batch waited for the node to hold each delivery for 5 s, but the queries it sent in that time could each take up to 30 s, with their waits for a rate-limit refusal on top, and an answer that came after the 5 s still counted as held. A batch of 64 slow submissions could hold the signer's submissions for about 31 minutes. Each query now ends with the wait, a late answer counts as not held, which ends the batch, and a batch sends no delivery after 60 s: the rest are submitted one at a time.
- The replies of the reads made once per final block, such as delivery checks, were kept for that block without a bound: while finality stalled, every new message the relayer checked added one, and a read that fell out of the 1,024 the endpoint watches left its reply behind. Replies are now kept only for watched reads and dropped with them, at most 1 MiB with their arguments, and the least recently asked read goes first.
- A contract query took its place in the pace before it waited for one of the 8 in-flight slots, and went out without looking at the pace or at rate-limit holds again: once slow queries finished, the queries waiting behind them went out at once (592 in 73 ms in a model of a relayer's catch-up), and a `429` hold set while they waited held none of them back. A query now takes its in-flight slot first, then its place in the pace, and goes out only once no hold of its class remains.
- A delivery was submitted with the gas limit the relayer's gas policy asked for, which follows the gas the sender paid for, with no ceiling: a payment above Rusk's block gas limit of 3,000,000,000 left the delivery in the mempool for good, since no block can hold it, and one above `gasLimit` raised what a failed delivery pays. A delivery's gas limit is now at most `gasLimit`, which a configuration may not set above the block gas limit.
- An event log that met a row below the block it was reading, from an archive serving rows out of block order, kept the blocks it had taken before, the incomplete block of that row included: once the archive was repaired, the log never read that block again, and its missing rows were lost. Restored rows were lost the same way: after an operator rebuilt an archive that had skipped a block, the log, which held the blocks around it, read past the restored rows. An event log now checks every block it holds that it reads again against its own rows, and every block it holds rows of must come again before a later block does; a row out of block order, or a block that differs, drops its rows from that block on, and it reads them again. Contract sync indexes deliveries by their sequence, so the delivery indexer now records every sequence below a count it reported, and every one it served, as published, and the log keeps the dropped rows of those sequences: where the rows it reads again would give a published sequence another delivery, it halts with a critical error naming the contract, topic, block and sequence, until the operator resets the agent's database and its `eventCursorDir` (README, Event log halts), instead of numbering them again. A delivery log the previous version wrote, which records no published sequences, counts every delivery it holds as published.
- An event log passed a block in which the contract emitted no row of the log's topic without checking it: no `checkBlock`, no check of its rows' block hash, and no bound by the node's last final block. A faulty archive row of another topic, such as one in block 2^32 under a hash that does not parse, became the height up to which the delivery log held every delivery, and the delivery indexer's cursor failed for good on a height past its u32 range. Every block is now checked like a block of the topic before the log passes it, and a row more than 64 blocks above the node's tip fails the read without moving the log.
- An event log read on to the end of the block it was in once its pages were used, so an endpoint that served one block without end held the log's lock and grew its memory until the agent ran out, again after every restart. A call now reads its pages and no more; a block not read whole is staged in `eventCursorDir` and read on from there by the next call, and only becomes entries once whole and checked. A block of more than 1,000,000 rows of the contract, or 32 MiB of rows of the topic, which no Rusk block holds, fails the log with an error.
- A relayer started while its Rusk node was unreachable dropped the Dusk chain until restart: building each Dusk component queried the deployment's identity, and after about 15 s of retries the relayer left the chain out of its origins and destinations. Dusk components now build without a request; each client checks the identity, once, before its first request, and requests fail without being sent until a check succeeds. The validator no longer retries construction, and its first reads wait for the node instead.
- `delivered` and a validator's startup check could count a delivery or announcement in a block that was accepted and rolled back entirely within the check's own requests: the blocks above the last final one, read up to a tip older than the state the check read, held no event of it. The relayer then marked the message processed for good, and the validator stopped announcing. A delivery or announcement now counts only once the agent's event log holds its event in a block `checkBlock(onlyFinalized)` accepts. Until then the check fails as not final, also while the log does not reach the last final block, cannot be read, or the client keeps none, which used to count the record once no block above the last final one held it. Where the log holds every final block, a delivery check names the non-final block that holds the delivery, or, when none does, says that its block was rolled back during the check or skipped by the node's archive, with a warning.
- A finalized block whose archive rows a Rusk node never wrote, which happens when its archive write fails, left the dispatch, Merkle hook and IGP indexers waiting for good, reporting the records as not archived yet. When contract state records events in such a block and the archive has finalized a later block of the contract, or the node's last final block at or above it (so the contract's last record before it falls idle is covered too), the lookup now fails with an error naming the block, its hash, the contract and the records, and the agent logs it as a critical error, at most every 10 minutes per block. Records are still accepted only with their rows.
- A block's archive rows were kept in memory without checking each row's block hash and transaction hash, and rows with data that is not hex were left out while the block still counted as read whole. A lookup of a row that did not parse then failed from memory on every retry, and a transaction whose row was left out was reported to have emitted nothing: the Mailbox and Merkle hook indexers returned no logs for it and the IGP indexer returned early. A block with such a row is now never kept; a lookup by transaction checks the block with `checkBlock` from memory as from the archive, and when the transaction's rows name no record, contract state decides whether the block holds its records. A cached block whose selected row fails to parse is dropped.
- A saved archive scan position was kept whenever the archive returned no row from it and had not finalized its row's block, which also happens when the position names another hash for that block (a row served under a forged block hash), when the node's ledger lacks the block, or when the archive has passed the block without finalizing it. Lookups from such a position failed for good. A position is now kept only while the ledger holds its block under its hash and the node has not finalized that block yet or the archive has not finalized the node's last final block either; otherwise it is dropped and the scan starts over.
- A relayed `process` was refused once its metadata and message passed 60,416 bytes, 1 KiB short of the 61,440 bytes of serialized arguments the `dusk-tx` helper accepts. The arguments are now serialized first and refused exactly when the helper would refuse them.
- A Dusk delivery counted only once its block was final, whoever delivered the message. The relayer used to mark a message processed as soon as the node's tip held its delivery, and never retried it: if finality stalled past the relayer's confirmation delay and a rollback dropped the delivery, the message was lost. While no final block held the delivery, `delivered` failed and the relayer checked again later; a rolled-back delivery read as not delivered, and the message was relayed again.
- A validator's startup waited until its announcement's block was final, instead of ending as soon as the node's tip held an announcement that a rollback could still drop.
- Checkpoint quorums counted only signatures over the same checkpoint, and message-ID multisig metadata only signatures over the message's own ID. A validator that signed the right root and index with another message ID used to join the honest signatures' group, which then failed to form one quorum and aborted the whole fetch: a validator that answered first could hold back delivery for good.
- A lookup read its whole block and kept the block's rows in memory, so other lookups in the block matched against them instead of reading the archive again. Progress through a block was saved as a position after the block, which another part of the same block could not use: with `index.chunk` 1, a cursor over 1,000 Merkle insertions in reverse archive order read 3,000 archive pages, and the Mailbox's 2,000 rows 5,500. Each now reads one pass, 5 and 10 pages. The cache keeps only blocks whose rows carry the hash `checkBlock(onlyFinalized)` accepted, at most 64 blocks and 64 MiB, and 32 MiB for one block.
- A transaction lookup by the Merkle hook, Mailbox and IGP indexers looked up only the records that the transaction's own archive rows name, instead of every record of its block. Contract sync looks up each dispatch's transaction, so a block of 1,000 dispatches cost the hook and Mailbox indexers about 2 million state reads each; it now costs 12,000.
- Parse errors were cut to 1 KiB before they went into errors and log lines, like the response bodies they come from. A parse error quotes the value it fails on, so a `finalizedEvents` page, account status or gas price reply with a 1 MiB string in a numeric field put the whole string into an error that contract sync logged on every retry. The `dusk-tx` helper's stdout, stderr and error text, up to 1 MiB each, are cut the same way.
- A saved archive scan position was reused only when its row is below the block the position names. A position that contradicts its own row was dropped and the scan started over, instead of resuming after that block's first rows and missing them on every retry.
- When the archive returned no row from a saved scan position's row on, and had not finalized that row's block yet, the lookup failed until the archive caught up and kept the position, instead of dropping the contract's positions and rescanning from its first row. An archive that had finalized the block without the row, or returned later rows without it, numbers its rows differently, and the scan still started over. Positions saved by the previous version lack the row's block, are dropped once, and the next scan of each contract starts from its first row.
- A `finalizedEvents` page over the agent's 4 MiB response limit was asked for again at the same position with half the rows, down to one, instead of failing at that position on every retry. 200 Mailbox rows exceed the limit once dispatched message bodies average more than about 20 KiB, and dispatch and delivery indexing stopped for good at the first such page.
- A lookup that ran ahead of the archive, between the node finalizing a block and the archive inserting its rows, kept its scan position instead of sending the next scan back to the contract's first row.
- The `process` simulation passed only on the encoding of the `()` that `process` returns. Any other success reply, such as a proxy's error page sent with status 200, failed the simulation instead of letting the transaction burn its gas limit.
- Response bodies were cut to 1 KiB before they went into errors or logs, instead of being copied in whole, up to 4 MiB.

- The relayer ran `process` as an unsigned contract query before submitting it and retried later when the query failed, instead of paying the full gas limit for a delivery that reverts.
